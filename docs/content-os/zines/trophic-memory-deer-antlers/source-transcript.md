If you take a deer and you take a knife and you etch one particular location on the branch structure, it makes a little callus, the bone heals. That year, the whole thing falls off, so it's gone. Next year, new antler rack comes up. They have an ectopic branch at the location where you made the damage. Amazing.

First of all, it has to remember where in the branch structure the damage was. Then the whole thing falls off anyway, so you can't store it locally. It has to be where in the scalp, somewhere else in the rest of the body. Then it has to be imprinted onto the new bone cells so that, okay, when you, you know, 2 cm forward, when you get there, take a right to like an extra branch.

He was always writing about this stuff, and I got a letter from Bubenic, and he said, "I have 35 years worth of these antlers in my garage, and I need to get rid of them. Do you want the antlers?" I said, "I sure as hell do." And he sent me 13 boxes, 13 big boxes of antlers. Every antler meticulously labeled the name of the deer. We had them cat scanned at the vet school.

No one's ever going to be able to do this again. You're never going to get money, you don't have time. A single experiment is at least 10 years. The standard tools that we have for explaining outcomes in morphogenesis are not amenable to this. We don't have the right tools yet.
