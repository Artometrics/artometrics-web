# Draft scaffold (discipline cover)

## Working dek

Everyone says “the narrative changed.” Almost nobody can define a narrative as an economic object. Robert Shiller’s narrative economics treats popular stories as contagious — with infection rates, mutations, and burnout — that move markets and institutions. This is the briefing note for culture workers who are tired of vibes language.

## Research question

What is a measurable economic narrative, how does it spread, and how should operators brief with it without becoming grifters?

## Core definitions (draft)

- **Economic narrative:** a contagious story that can affect decisions (spend, invest, cancel, hire, silence).  
- **Contagion:** growth in tellings/retellings (news volume, search, social) that behaves like an epidemic curve.  
- **Mutation:** slogan/variant that keeps the story alive (*“box office poison,” “everyone knew,” “too big to fail”*).  
- **Burnout:** decline in new infections even if the underlying fact remains true.

## Four worked cases (tiles)

1. **Crypto boom/bust narratives** — “digital gold” → “tulip” flip  
2. **Pandemic narratives** — reopen / forever / lab / natural (measure volume, not truth)  
3. **Meme stocks** — retail story as coordination device  
4. **Celebrity ruin narratives** — how “why not sooner?” itself becomes a contagious frame (bridge to Protection Threshold)
