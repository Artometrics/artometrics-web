<h2 id="singer" class="anchored">A HIT IS NOT REHAB</h2>
<p class="art-p"><strong>Question:</strong> Does Bohemian Rhapsody’s success mean Bryan Singer was back — or only that DGA credit outlived a firing? Target keyword: <em>Bryan Singer Bohemian Rhapsody credit</em>.</p>
<p class="art-p">Working position: Singer was fired from <em>Bohemian Rhapsody</em> (Dec 2017) yet retained DGA credit; the film’s success does <strong>not</strong> equal a return to studio power. The Atlantic (2019) marks a clearer mainstream-attachment collapse. A later fringe return is not rehab.</p>

<h2 id="limitations" class="anchored">LIMITATIONS</h2>
<p class="art-p">Allegations are not convictions. Flops may precede, follow, or be unrelated to exposés. Selection bias: we study the eventually exposed. Journalists also time stories. Working gross/budget ratios in <code>leto-film-book.csv</code> are trade-reported bands — label observed vs derived. Charts remain PLACEHOLDER until Plotly/PNG exhibits ship. Ethics pass required before <code>cos:publish</code>.</p>
<h2 id="conclusion" class="anchored">CONCLUSION</h2>
<p class="art-p">The best reading is modest: test whether institutional protection tracks commercial and risk thresholds — dual books, pre-decline studios, false rehab signals, and broadcaster trust — then check sources before turning any index into a claim. This scaffold is not a published report.</p>
