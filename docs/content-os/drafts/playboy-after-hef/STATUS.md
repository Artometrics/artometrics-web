## Working dek

Playboy sold a magazine, then a mansion, then a feeling of access. After Hugh Hefner’s death, the company tried to become pure intellectual property — licensing, experiences, a magazine reboot — while the founder’s late-life reputation hardened into a liability the brand still wears. In 2024 the board rejected Cooper Hefner’s $100M+ bid to buy “the family’s legacy” back. The question isn’t whether the rabbit logo still reads. It’s **what myth a licensing shell is allowed to sell** in a culture that now charts age-gap power in public.

## Research question

When a lifestyle brand’s founder *was* the product, what survives him — hospitality, media, or a hollow mark — and how does the market price that afterlife?
